# Owner's groundwork (pasted 2026-11-05, verbatim in substance)
1. Mana tied to Karmic Energy. Positive karma plentiful, low mana. Negative karma legally harder to accumulate, much more mana. Both give mana proportionate to the deed. Mana potential usually by racial traits, genetics a big factor. Anybody can use magic; even the least potent some. Mana is universal for all magic types.
 Magic: tune mana to a trait in the physical world and 'will' it. With tuned mana, will it into anything with the creative power and proportionate mana. To tune: vividly imagine what you want, replay it over and over until you convince the mana to make it reality, using a limb (or catalyst) to stream mana to surroundings/target. Anything you can realistically do with mana reserved. Example: flood thoughts with a tree igniting; outstretch limb/tool to channel.
 Using magic can create a feedback loop generating karmic energy; cost almost always outweighs gain. Certain negative deeds (needless murder) give net positive mana depending on the method of killing magic.
2. Land = Aumadyr. Direplains home to Dire (animal people, generally large, traits of their species). Dire born when a pregnant/gravid animal has high mana potential; the litter grows into Dire. Dire x same species = pure Dire. Dire x other races = Dire-snubs (half breed, frowned upon by most if not all races), demi-humans. "Snub/Snubfolk" is a slur for half breeds of all types.
 Animar: mining city, hierarchy of necromancers. Citizens carry 'donor' badges consenting to skeletons reanimated for free labour; generates hefty negative karma but for a positive cause; citizens content, way of life.
3. Jorikzalt: Dwarven empire. Classic short and stout dwarves. Women higher mana potential, men stronger. Mining and trading primary; smithing common, higher tiers reserved for gifted or royalty. Notable: Heahmund.
4. Elves descend directly from trolls. Ridiculously high metabolism and regeneration; very vulnerable to cold. Live in warm climates, mainly volcanoes (comfort + fuel); can eat coal and other usually inedible things. Tinkerers: comparatively modern designs (early guns, steam contraptions). Great mechanists (survive failed tests). Not the highest mana potential.
 Trolls: ancestors/lingering relatives; larger, bulkier, less civilized; incredibly talented mechanics/engineers who pioneered most elven tech, many inventions lost or hidden in ancient ruins of their pinnacle cities. Loved silver for architecture, tools, inventions. Silver = neutral metal with strong karmic conductivity: holds mana, or in dangerously talented hands recreates it. Trolls have one of the lowest mana potentials ever, almost entirely hands-on; some silver inventions found workarounds. Ashen skin, brutish lankier elves, patterns/tattoos mostly not understood. Females barely different from males except breasts and more feminine features.
5. Humans: a mix of elves and dwarves. Sturdy, inventive, more grounded in nature. Decent mana all round (better than elves and male dwarves, worse than many races). Impeccable strategists and leaders, rally people for a common cause. Live in resource-rich areas, settle away from other human kingdoms, forming their own adaptations.
6. Goblins: live in or near caves; insane adaptability, anywhere with trees; appearance (skin colour) from the colour of the blooming leaves in their environment (latent magic subconsciously wills skin to the leaves they adore). Tribalist, revere trees and nature, NOT aggressively primal. INSANE mana potential; ridiculously high positive karma from intrinsic care of nature. Caves lush with preferred trees and flora; mana mainly used to terraform caves; could be comedically dangerous sorcerers in combat. Cut down trees for homes but hold a funeral for the tree first.
7. Seladrak: lizard-like stone-dieting cave dwellers (see Vez'nan entry, same text): resemble reptile Dire, wide variety of sizes; eat stones, gems, earthen material; size by mineral density, upper limit unknown; rocky scaleless skin like their main mineral, natural flesh inside; no natural genders or reproductive organs; choose physical identity subconsciously as they feed (masc/fem, breasts, muscles); may form genitals upon learning of them; voices/personalities don't change with bodies. Asexual: crystal forms at their centre as density rises; must tear it out and embed it in their main mineral; a new Seladrak grows, resembling the parent at first; parents optional, some form pseudo families; not tearing it out = implode; with age they struggle to remove it; natural death = painless implosion when they can no longer reproduce. Negligible mana, not creative with it.
8. Seladrak civilizations large, spanning cave systems; blocky Mayan/Sunken City architecture; great respect for architecture (never eat stonework). Trade isn't their forte (they'd eat metal coins); trade non-earthen goods (leather, fabric, flora). Love clothes. Non-violent, rarely squabble; LOVE learning about and meeting other races; numbers and strength make them a serious potential threat.
9. A creature/troll machine with no concept of language: words spoken to and around it are misinterpreted as spell chants; based on length, tone and language it casts the spell it believes the words would cast. Unbelievably powerful and dangerous but braindead easy to avoid or exploit.
10. Heahmund: notable elderly dwarf warrior, son of a legendary blacksmith; trained all his life in smithing alongside elder brother Leitur. Dwarves went to war with a distant elven kingdom in neighbouring mountains. Heahmund, too inexperienced for war, crafted an impeccable armour for Leitur, who trusted it fully. War was grisly and short (~1 year); dwarves vastly outnumbered the elves, but elven modern weaponry made traditional armour seldom effective. Leitur died. Heahmund, devastated, blamed himself, threw away his craft, vowed never to smith again, thinking had Leitur used his own armour he'd be alive. Self-exiled, left his kingdom and family, no destination, travels to help those in need personally. Long white hair, full braided beard with metal rings, old for a dwarf, aged face, grey eyes. Leather armour with some plating picked up from smiths he met (not his own craft). Heavy rune-crested warhammer, no shield.
11. Many Dire societies focus on bones: armour, weapons, tools, structures, currency. Bones from killed opponents or digested prey are cleaned and sanitised. Specialised bone-crafters make bone gear rivalling steel; most middle-tier metals are stronger, but bone's availability makes replacement easy. Skulls preserved as ornaments, armour, masks, trophies. Coin is the closest universal currency, but some tribes/kingdoms trade in bones; small weak bones less valuable, femurs/vertebrae/sturdy bones more; rates vary heavily.
